# Project Log

## Early November 2026 — Choosing My Topic

When I started thinking about my DSA210 project, I wanted to work with something personal rather than choosing a random dataset online.

I've been tracking my sleep on my iPhone for years, so I already had quite a lot of data. I thought about analyzing my screen time, especially TikTok and Instagram, but I was also interested in how my university schedule might be related to my sleeping habits.

This semester, I'm taking courses including IE305, IE311, IE451, MATH204, and DSA210. My schedule changes a lot throughout the week. For example, I have a class at 8:40 AM on Wednesdays, while on other days my classes start later.

I decided to focus on whether these differences, along with exam periods, are reflected in my sleep records.

## Mid-November 2026 — Collecting and Reviewing Data

I chose September 28, 2026, the beginning of the fall semester, as the starting point for my main dataset.

I started organizing my iPhone sleep records and comparing them with my weekly class schedule and exam calendar.

November was an especially interesting period because I had several exams close together, including IE451, MATH204, IE311, IE305, and DSA210. I also have upcoming MATH204 and IE311 exams in December.

While reviewing my sleep records, I noticed that some dates appeared more than once. I decided to use the most recently listed record for each date and keep track of the duplicates rather than treating them as separate nights.

I also collected the Screen Time summaries available on my iPhone. However, these are mostly weekly summaries, so I still need to check how they can be used alongside the daily sleep records.

## November 24, 2026 — Setting Up GitHub

I created this repository to keep my project organized and document my progress throughout the semester.

So far, I have identified my data sources, selected the main research questions, and started reviewing the sleep records for missing dates and duplicate entries.

I also considered including my summer internship and holiday periods. For now, I decided to focus on the fall semester so that the main analysis has a consistent academic context. I may use the summer records later for a separate comparison.
